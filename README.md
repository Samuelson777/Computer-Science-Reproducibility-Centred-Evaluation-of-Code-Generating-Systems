## Abstract

Computer science is a foundational scientific and engineering discipline concerned with computation, information processing, algorithms, data structures, systems, software, and intelligent behavior. As the field expands into artificial intelligence, distributed systems, cybersecurity, human-centered computing, and automated software generation, the need for rigorous, reproducible, and ethically responsible research becomes increasingly important.

This paper provides a structured review of computer science foundations and modern developments, with a focused discussion on code-generating large language models and benchmark-based evaluation. It proposes a reproducibility-centred evaluation perspective that emphasizes official benchmark releases, controlled prompting, fixed decoding policies, sandboxed execution, strengthened test oracles, and transparent reporting.

## Key Findings

* Computer science is no longer limited to programming or algorithms; it now supports nearly every modern scientific, industrial, and social system.
* Artificial intelligence and code-generation systems are changing how software is written, tested, and maintained.
* Benchmark-based evaluation is useful but can be misleading if datasets, prompts, execution environments, and scoring methods are not reported clearly.
* Reproducibility is essential for trustworthy computational research.
* Future computer science research must balance technical innovation with security, ethics, sustainability, and human benefit.

---

## Conclusion

Computer science remains one of the most important disciplines of the modern era because it provides the theoretical, technical, and practical foundations for digital systems, intelligent machines, secure communication, scientific computing, and global information infrastructure. From algorithms and data structures to artificial intelligence and distributed computing, the field continues to expand in both complexity and social importance.

This research paper concludes that the future of computer science depends not only on creating faster algorithms, more powerful models, and larger systems, but also on ensuring that computational methods are reliable, reproducible, ethical, and beneficial to society. In particular, the evaluation of code-generating systems must move beyond simple benchmark scores toward transparent protocols that include controlled prompts, fixed decoding policies, secure execution, stronger test oracles, and detailed reproducibility reports.

Overall, computer science should be understood as both a technical discipline and a human-centered scientific field. Its progress will depend on responsible innovation, interdisciplinary collaboration, open research practices, and a continued commitment to solving real-world problems through computation.
